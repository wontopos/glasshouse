# Governance

## Who administers

Administrators are companies that **use** memory rather than sell it: product teams
whose agents depend on a memory layer. They have no stake in which vendor wins, only
in whether the numbers are true.

Wontopos is the exception. It hosts this and keeps it running, and it sells memory.
That is a conflict and it is named here rather than argued away.

This rule can change. It is what the administrators think is fair today, not a
constitution.

## The fourteen days

Wontopos sees every version before anyone else, because it hosts them. So it does not
get to use that.

When a version is published, Wontopos publishes no result on it for fourteen days.
The rule applies to Wontopos alone. No other company waits.

## What the administrator does

Hosts the repository, keeps it running, and merges what the rules admit.

## What the administrator does not do

- Decide who is admitted. The rules do.
- Merge its own submissions.
- Change a rule alone. Rule changes go through the same process as submissions.
- Remove a submission that meets the rules, including one that beats its own.

If any of these is violated, the licence lets you take the whole thing and run it
elsewhere. That is the guarantee. The rest is a promise.

## Adding a benchmark version, a rule, or a result

**From a company.** Open a pull request. Three other companies listed here state
their agreement on it. When the third agrees, it merges.

**From an individual.** Open a pull request. There is no threshold, on purpose.

**Objections.** An objection has to name what is wrong: a number that does not
recompute, a prompt that is not the one that ran, a rule that would admit something
unreproducible. "I disagree" is not an objection. Objections are answered in public
on the pull request.

## Drafting a version

A version of the benchmark is drafted by companies. Not every company drafts.

A version, once published, does not change. Comments and pull requests against it are
voted on, and what the vote picks goes into the next version.

## Conflicts of interest

Every person merging declares who they work for, in the pull request. A submission
from the same company is merged by someone else, or waits.

## Removing something

A submission is removed if the record turns out not to support the number and the
submitter cannot correct it. The removal and the reason stay in the repository.
